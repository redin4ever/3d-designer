## (a) Gaps — taxonomy

These are seven distinct defects with different math and different fixes. Treating them as one "fix the gaps" step is the most common mistake.

| Defect | Formal definition | Detection | Repair | Library/API that already does it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boundary hole | A closed loop of edges each bounding exactly 1 face (missing patch) | Walk edges with face-count 1 | Triangulate the boundary loop | `bmesh.ops.holes_fill`, `bpy.ops.mesh.fill_holes(sides=0)`, `trimesh.repair.fill_holes()` (fan; small holes only — bad on non-convex holes **observed** [trimesh.repair docs](https://trimesh.org/trimesh.repair.html)), PyMeshLab "Close Holes" filter, `pymeshfix`/MeshFix (produces single watertight mesh, removes self-intersections/degenerate elements too — **observed** [pymeshfix](https://github.com/pyvista/pymeshfix)), CGAL `Polygon_mesh_processing::stitch_borders` for duplicated-but-matching borders (**observed** [CGAL PMP repair](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Polygon_mesh_processing/group__PMP__repairing__grp.html)) |
| Non-manifold edge | Edge with face-count ≠ 2 (0 handled as loose, 1 = boundary, ≥3 = non-manifold fan) | `bpy.ops.mesh.select_non_manifold`, Open3D `is_edge_manifold()` (param `allow_boundary_edges`) | Split fan / remove extra faces / re-triangulate junction | Blender 3D-Print Toolbox "Non Manifold" check (**observed** [print3d_toolbox wiki](https://projects.blender.org/extensions/print3d_toolbox/wiki/Analyze)), Open3D, PyMeshLab `repair_non_manifold_edges` |
| Non-manifold vertex | Vertex whose incident-face "star" is not a single edge-connected fan (two cones touching only at a point) | Open3D `is_vertex_manifold()` | Duplicate the vertex, one copy per fan | Open3D, MeshLib |
| Duplicate/loose vertices | Vertices at (near-)identical position not welded; or vertices/edges with 0 attached faces | `bpy.ops.mesh.select_loose`; distance test for dupes | Weld coincident verts; delete loose | `bmesh.ops.remove_doubles`, `bpy.ops.mesh.remove_doubles` (**note:** both fail to merge *exact* duplicates when threshold ≈0 — open Blender bug **observed** [#132038](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/132038)) |
| Flipped/inconsistent normals | Face winding order not consistently outward relative to neighbors | 3D-Print Toolbox "Bad Contiguous Edges" (neighbor normals disagree — **observed** [print3d_toolbox wiki](https://projects.blender.org/extensions/print3d_toolbox/wiki/Analyze)) | Recompute winding from a seed face outward | `bpy.ops.mesh.normals_make_consistent(inside=False)`, `trimesh.repair.fix_normals()` |
| Self-intersection | Two non-adjacent triangles share points in 3D (excluding shared-vertex/edge adjacency) | BVH broad-phase + triangle-triangle test (Möller/Devillers-style, coplanar handled separately) | Remesh/cut at intersection curve | `libigl` `remesh_self_intersections()` (known to leave residual cases — **observed** [libigl#760](https://github.com/libigl/libigl/issues/760)), Open3D `is_self_intersecting()` / `get_self_intersecting_triangles()`, `PyMesh.resolve_self_intersection()`, 3D-Print Toolbox "Intersecting Faces" check |
| Disconnected shells | ≥2 connected components in the face-adjacency graph, possibly each individually watertight | Connected-components on face graph | Join, or leave separate if intentional (bolt + nut) | `trimesh.split(only_watertight=...)`, `Trimesh.body_count`; Blender `bpy.ops.mesh.separate(type='LOOSE')` (inverse op) |
| **Visual gap** (part A doesn't reach part B — a stair tread short of the stringer) | *Not* a mesh-validity defect at all — both parts can be individually perfect manifolds. It is a **placement/dimensioning** error: distance between two solids' surfaces exceeds design tolerance | Not caught by any watertight/manifold check. Requires BVH nearest-point or bounding-volume gap query between the two parts | Extend/reposition one part, or bridge with new geometry | No standard library does this automatically — it needs the generator to know "tread must span joist-to-joist" as a semantic constraint. **inferred** |

**Formal manifold/watertight definitions** (**observed**, consistent across [Open3D](https://open3d.org/docs/latest/tutorial/Basic/mesh.html), [David Stutz](https://davidstutz.de/a-formal-definition-of-watertight-meshes/), [ManifoldPlus paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11621)):
- **Edge-manifold**: every edge bounds exactly 1 (boundary) or 2 (interior) triangles.
- **Vertex-manifold**: the triangles around each vertex form a single connected fan, not multiple fans touching only at that point.
- **Watertight** = edge-manifold **and** vertex-manifold **and** no boundary edges (every edge has exactly 2 faces) **and** not self-intersecting. Watertight implies a well-defined interior/exterior (needed for booleans, 3D printing, physics).

## (b) Realism and detail

**Why AI output reads as "glued boxes":** the recurring, cross-source diagnosis is not one thing but a stack of omissions: (1) perfectly sharp CAD-like edges with no bevel, (2) topology that is literally separate primitive volumes touching at a face rather than a continuous surface, (3) uniform/flat material response, (4) no micro-surface variation (scratches, panel lines, wear), (5) proportions that don't reference a real-world scale unit. Practitioner sources for 2026-era generators specifically flag "melted/blobby" detail where under-described regions get smoothed away, and note that professional-grade evaluation in 2026 (vs. 2024 tolerance) explicitly penalizes messy topology and non-manifold geometry, not just silhouette (**observed** [MakeIt3D](https://makeit3d.app/guides/fix-ai-generated-3d-model-mesh), [Medium/Meshy review](https://medium.com/illumination/meshy-ai-3d-generator-review-2026-full-production-pipeline-tested-298b006b4a42)).

**Leverage-ranked fixes:**

1. **Bevel/chamfer every real edge.** Practitioner consensus, repeated across multiple independent sources, is that this is the single highest-leverage change: "no edge in reality is infinitely sharp"; a sharp CAD edge produces a hard, artificial specular break, while even a tiny bevel produces the highlight line and soft shading transition the eye reads as "real material" (**observed** [Tripo3D](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/hd-model-edge-sharpness-and-bevel-realism), [Novedge/C4D](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/cinema-4d-tip-beveling-best-practices-for-realistic-hard-surface-modeling)). **I could not find a controlled quantitative study measuring "realism score" vs. bevel presence** — the claim is strong, repeated, cross-source practitioner consensus, not a peer-reviewed measurement. Treat the *direction* as verified, the *magnitude* as inferred. Caveat found in the same sources: beveling *everything uniformly* is itself a tell — only edges that would plausibly wear/catch light should be beveled, with width scaled to the object's real-world size.
2. Edge-loop/topology quality: quads for anything that deforms or subdivides; n-gons only on flat, non-subdividing regions (walls, tabletops) or high-poly bake-only meshes — n-gons subdivide unpredictably and cause shading artifacts; triangles acceptable as one-off fills but 3-valence poles densify and 5+-valence poles sparsify under subdivision, causing pinching (**observed** [TurboSquid topology guide](https://resources.turbosquid.com/training/modeling/tris-quads-n-gons/)).
3. Greebling/kitbashing for surface complexity: small relief details ("greebles") sourced by kitbashing (repurposing kit/prop geometry) break up large flat/blank areas; the cited principle is to mix greebled zones with smooth "resting" zones rather than greeble uniformly (**observed** [Wikipedia/Greeble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeble)).
4. Material response: avoid pure-black/pure-white albedo — practitioner PBR charts put non-metal albedo at roughly sRGB 60–240 and metal albedo at sRGB 180–250 (never 0 or 255), because no real material reflects 0% or 100% of light; roughness should vary across a surface (uniform roughness reads as "plastic/CG"); Fresnel is handled automatically by the BRDF and doesn't need manual authoring (**observed** [PBR albedo guide](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/pbr-albedo-values-guide-lighting-artists-only-ron-haimov), [Marmoset PBR](https://marmoset.co/posts/physically-based-rendering-and-you-can-too/)).
5. Lighting vs. geometry: lighting can only reveal geometric detail that exists (via specular highlights, occlusion) — it cannot substitute for missing bevels/greebles/edge-loops. **inferred**, but consistent with how the bevel/specular-highlight mechanism above is described.

**Computable quality metrics (flagging which are machine-checkable today):**

| Metric | Computable now? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Manifold/watertight/self-intersection status | **Yes** | Open3D `is_edge_manifold`, `is_vertex_manifold`, `is_self_intersecting`; trimesh `is_watertight` |
| Triangle aspect ratio / quality | **Yes** | `q = (m²+n²+l²)/(4√3·S)`, 1.0 = equilateral, →∞ = degenerate (**observed** [mesh quality metrics](https://www.mechead.com/mesh-quality-checking-ansys-workbench/)) |
| Edge-length uniformity | **Yes** | Coefficient of variation of edge lengths per mesh; cheap, no library wraps it directly — trivial to compute from vertex/edge arrays. **inferred** as a proxy, not found as a named published "realism" metric |
| Polygon budget vs. asset class | **Yes** | Compare tri count to class table below |
| Curvature distribution / surface roughness | **Yes, with cost** | Discrete curvature (e.g. per-vertex mean/Gaussian curvature) computable via libigl/Open3D; used in perceptual-quality research (Standard Deviation of Curvature Difference, curvature-tensor metrics) (**observed** [curvature-tensor quality metric](https://www.gipsa-lab.grenoble-inp.fr/~kai.wang/papers/MGV2014.pdf)) |
| Silhouette complexity | **Yes, view-dependent** | Defined in aesthetics research as edge count of the projected silhouette from one or many views (**observed** [aesthetic preferences paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12373)) — needs a chosen camera set, so it's a design choice, not a single number |
| "Has bevels" / sharp-edge ratio | **Partially** | No standard metric found; can be approximated as fraction of mesh edges with dihedral angle inside a "sharp" band that also carry a bevel loop, but this is **inferred**, not a published metric |
| Overall "looks realistic" score | **No** | No general-purpose automatic realism scorer found; perceptual mesh-quality papers exist (2102.03982) but target compression/simplification artifacts, not generative-AI blockiness specifically |

Polygon budgets by class (**observed**, industry blog synthesis, treat as rule-of-thumb not standard): mobile/background props 100–800 tris; mobile hero character <5,000 tris; console/PC background objects 1,000–10,000 tris; console/PC hero character 15,000–50,000 tris (LOD0 for cinematics can run 80,000–150,000) (**observed** [cgaxis polycount guide](https://cgaxis.com/polygon-count-guide-how-many-polys-do-you-really-need-in-2026/), [neural4d](https://blog.neural4d.com/user-guide/polygon-count-for-3d-game-assets-printing-and-webar/)). Human proportion anchor: realistic adult figures are commonly modeled at 7–7.5 head-heights; the idealized "8 heads" canon is a stylization choice, not a realism target (**observed** [Anatomy4Sculptors](https://anatomy4sculptors.com/blog/about-human-proportions-calculator/)).

## (c) Angles, spaces, units

| Concept | Convention / API | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation representation | `Object.rotation_mode` enum: `'XYZ'`/`'XZY'`/`'YXZ'`/`'YZX'`/`'ZXY'`/`'ZYX'` (Euler, 6 orders), `'QUATERNION'`, `'AXIS_ANGLE'`; `Object.rotation_euler`, `.rotation_quaternion`, `.rotation_axis_angle` all coexist on the object but only the mode-selected one drives the transform (**observed** [object_rotation_mode_items](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy_types_enum_items/object_rotation_mode_items.html)) | All 6 Euler orders are gimbal-lock-prone (two axes align, one DOF lost); quaternions have no gimbal lock but only interpolate correctly within 180°; `Quaternion.to_euler()` accepts an order, but `Euler.to_quaternion()` does not round-trip the order automatically — a known scripting foot-gun (**observed** [BlenderArtists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/euler-to-quaternion-and-back-but-with-respect-to-rotation-mode/1340107)) |
| Local / global / parent space | `matrix_basis` (edit-buffer transform) → `matrix_local` (relative to parent) → `matrix_world` (world-space, includes parent chain) | Reading/writing the wrong matrix silently produces double-transforms under a parent |
| Snapping / constraints | Snap-to-vertex/edge/face, `Copy Rotation`/`Track To`/`Damped Track` constraints | — |
| Axis-aligned vs. arbitrary plane | Custom Transform Orientations (align to face/edge) | Angle tools default to global axes unless orientation explicitly switched |
| Handedness / up-axis | **Blender: Z-up, right-handed.** **glTF: Y-up, right-handed**, mapped from Blender as X→X, Y→−Z, Z→Y on export (**observed** [Verge3D conventions](https://www.soft8soft.com/docs/manual/en/programmers_guide/Conventions.html)). **Unity: Y-up but left-handed** — importers like UnityGLTF must flip the X axis (apply a −1,1,1 scale) and additionally correct rotation sign, since a positive rotation is CW in Unity vs. CCW in glTF (**observed** [UnityGLTF#257](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/issues/257)) | This chain (Blender Z-up right-handed → glTF Y-up right-handed → Unity Y-up left-handed) is a real, repeatedly-reported, constant bug source — mirroring/flipping that only appears after a full export→import round trip |
| Units / scale | Scene `unit_settings.scale_length` (aka Unit Scale): 1.0 = 1 Blender Unit = 1 metre; 0.01 = 1 BU = 1 cm (default new-file value is 1.0 metric in current Blender; older defaults/add-ons sometimes ship 0.01) (**observed** [Blender scene units](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/scene_layout/scene/properties.html)) | Mixed unit_scale across imported assets makes real-world dimension checks (e.g., railing code minimums, §(d)) silently wrong even though the viewport "looks right" |
| Applied vs. unapplied transform | `Ctrl+A → Scale` (Object Mode) bakes object-level scale into mesh data, resetting scale to (1,1,1) | Unapplied (especially non-uniform) scale distorts Bevel modifier width, gives wrong results from Boolean, and produces "bizarre and unpredictable" physics because the physics/modifier evaluation reads the un-corrected object-space geometry against a mismatched scale transform (**observed** [Blender bevel/apply-scale writeups](https://braxtonwise.com/blender-uneven-bevels-apply-scale/)) |
| Angle between two faces | `dot(n1, n2)` → `acos()`, range [0, π]; exposed directly as the Geometry Nodes **Edge Angle** node, and in Mesh Analysis overlay ("Face Info: Angle") (**observed** [Edge Angle Node docs](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/mesh/read/edge_angle.html)) | Signed vs. unsigned angle (convex vs. concave dihedral) requires an extra sign test using the shared edge direction — not given directly by the dot product alone |
| "Make parallel/perpendicular/coplanar" | No single built-in operator; achieved via a Transform Orientation aligned to the target face + rotate/align, or third-party tools (Mesh Align Plus creates a "MAPlus" orientation from the destination face) (**observed** [Mesh Align Plus](https://github.com/egtwobits/mesh_mesh_align_plus/releases/tag/v0.3.0)) | This is a genuine gap in stock bpy — worth flagging as something to build rather than assume exists |

## (d) Clipping / interpenetration — and the railing worked example

**Detection.** Broad-phase pair pruning via BVH (`mathutils.bvhtree.BVHTree.FromObject`/`FromBMesh`, `.overlap(other_tree)` → list of overlapping triangle-index pairs, with an `epsilon` tolerance parameter — **observed** [mathutils.bvhtree docs](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/mathutils.bvhtree.html)); exact cut/intersection via `bmesh.ops.intersect` / `bmesh.ops.intersect_boolean`; in Python outside Blender, `trimesh`'s `CollisionManager` (backed by FCL, the Flexible Collision Library) reports contact and, where available, penetration depth. **Known limitation:** general non-convex mesh-mesh penetration depth is a hard problem — FCL frequently returns 0 depth on genuine mesh-mesh contact rather than a true value; robust penetration-depth numbers typically require at least one shape to be convex (**observed** [FCL issue #574](https://github.com/flexible-collision-library/fcl/issues/574), [trimesh.collision docs](https://trimesh.org/trimesh.collision.html)).

**Intended vs. unintended intersection.** No library or algorithm distinguishes these automatically — geometric overlap alone can't tell "post socketed 5cm into a deck" (correct) from "two posts overlapping by 5cm" (wrong); both are equal-magnitude BVH overlaps. This requires semantic context the generator must supply (a "socket depth" tag or a same-part-class check), not a pure geometry query. **inferred.**

**Boolean robustness.** Blender exposes three solvers with different failure modes: **Fast** (legacy, float-based, can fail/produce holes on perfectly overlapping or non-manifold geometry — good for fast block-out only); **Exact** (exact-arithmetic, handles messy/coplanar geometry far better, standard for production but slower — still has a documented failure on nearly-coplanar faces, [T82736](https://developer.blender.org/T82736)); the newer **Manifold**-library-backed solver (uses symbolic perturbation to guarantee manifold output *given* manifold input, but is float-based and does not work on non-manifold input, and has its own open coplanar-face bug as of the 4.5 cycle — **observed** [#140590](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/140590)). None of the three is unconditionally correct; the practical rule is Fast for iteration, Exact/Manifold for anything shipped, with a manifoldness pre-check gating which solver is even valid.

**Gap-vs-overlap tolerance.** Needs an epsilon defined in real units (scaled by scene `unit_settings.scale_length`, §(c)) — a tolerance in raw Blender Units is meaningless across differently-scaled assets. No library sets this for you; it's a pipeline decision.

### Railing worked example

A correct railing assembly, decomposed geometrically:
- **Posts**: instances at regular spacing along a path, each vertical (world +Z) regardless of the path's local slope — *not* perpendicular to the path tangent, which is the single most common naive-generator error on stairs (posts naively normal to the stair rake lean at the stair angle instead of standing vertical).
- **Top rail**: a swept profile that *follows the path* (constant offset above the walking surface / tread nosing) and must terminate exactly at each post's centerline, not at arbitrary parametric intervals that miss the post.
- **Bottom rail and balusters**: balusters fill the space between top and bottom rail at a spacing that satisfies a clear-opening constraint (below); their vertical member must also stay plumb through slope changes.
- **Corners**: posts must be inserted exactly at direction changes (not just at the regular pitch), and the two adjoining rail segments must miter or overlap at that shared post — a fixed-pitch array with no corner-aware re-sampling produces either a gap or a doubled/overlapping baluster at the turn.
- **Slope/stair transitions**: where the path changes from level to raked (stairs) or vice versa, the rail direction changes discontinuously at the "starting easing" — naive constant-offset extrusion produces a visible kink or a floating rail segment if post height isn't recomputed per-post from the local walking-surface elevation.

**Correct primitive:** array-along-curve / instancing-on-curve, not manual per-instance placement — Blender's Array modifier (Relative Offset / Fit Curve) combined with a Curve modifier (object follows a Bezier/poly path), or Geometry Nodes "Instance on Points" over a resampled curve, is the right building block; dedicated fence/railing Geometry-Node tools already exist as prior art (e.g. "Blender Fence Blocking Tool") (**observed** [Blender Fence Blocking Tool](https://onemorerat.gumroad.com/l/fence), [Array+Curve tutorial](https://nebmotion.co.uk/quicktip001/)). Naive per-instance copy-paste is what produces uneven spacing and posts that don't land exactly at corners.

**Real dimensions as realism anchors** (**observed**, U.S. model codes — IRC = residential, IBC = commercial; other jurisdictions differ, flag as a locale decision):
- Stair handrail height: 34–38 in above the tread nosing.
- Guard (barrier) height: ≥36 in residential (IRC), typically ≥42 in commercial (IBC).
- Baluster/guard opening: must not pass a 4-inch sphere (IRC §R312.1.3); practical builder target is ~3.5 in clear spacing for tolerance margin.
(**observed** [DataDrivenAEC IBC guide](https://datadrivenaec.com/insights/ibc-guardrail-handrail-requirements), [ViewRail 4" sphere rule](https://resources.viewrail.com/code-compliance/railing-code/4-sphere-rule))

## Formats and materials

| Format | Preserves | Destroys / lacks | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **glTF/GLB** | Geometry, UVs, PBR metal-rough materials + textures, skeleton/skinning, basic animation, scene graph, real-time-renderer-ready; single-file GLB | Procedural/modifier stacks, non-metal-rough material models (specular-glossiness is a deprecated extension), some advanced shading (subsurface/clearcoat often lossy) | **Exchange** to game engines / web / AR viewers (**observed** [Meshy format guide](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/meshy-export-formats-glb-fbx-obj-stl-guide)) |
| **USD/USDZ** | Full scene graph, layering/variants/references, materials, rigging; USDZ = Apple AR Quick Look package | Heavier tooling requirement, less universal DCC support than FBX/glTF historically | **Archive / large-scene interchange**, film & AR pipelines |
| **FBX** | Geometry, embedded textures/materials, full skeleton + skinning + animation "takes", cameras, lights | Proprietary (Autodesk), some interop quirks between exporting/importing DCCs (axis, unit, material-model mismatches) | **Rigged/animated character exchange** between DCC and game engine |
| **OBJ** | Geometry, UVs; materials only via a separate, loosely-referenced `.mtl` | No rig, no animation, no PBR standard, no scene graph | Lowest-common-denominator static-geometry exchange |
| **STL** | Triangle geometry only | No UV, no material/color (base spec), no rig | 3D printing only |
| **.blend** | Everything — modifiers, node graphs, drivers, full scene | Not an exchange format; requires Blender to open | **Working/native archive**, not delivery |

PBR: glTF's default material model is **metal-rough** — `baseColorTexture`/`baseColorFactor` (sRGB, alpha in A channel), `metallicRoughnessTexture` (roughness in G, metalness in B, packed together), plus normal/occlusion/emissive maps; factors and textures can combine multiplicatively (**observed** [Khronos glTF 2.0 spec](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html)). The alternative specular-glossiness model exists only as the `KHR_materials_pbrSpecularGlossiness` extension and is not core/guaranteed-supported — new pipelines should standardize on metal-rough.

## The expensive-to-discover-late list

1. **Unapplied non-uniform object scale.** Looks fine in the viewport; silently corrupts Bevel width, Boolean results, and physics once those are added later. Cheap to prevent (apply scale before handoff), expensive to diagnose after the fact because the mesh data itself looks correct.
2. **Up-axis/handedness mismatch across the export chain** (Blender Z-up right-handed → glTF Y-up right-handed → Unity Y-up left-handed). Invisible until a full export→import round trip; then it's mirrored geometry or inverted rotations, discovered downstream in a different tool than the one that caused it.
3. **Non-manifold/self-intersecting geometry that "looks fine" in the 3D viewport** but fails a slicer, a boolean op, or a physics bake. Blender's viewport does not warn by default — needs an explicit validation gate (3D-Print Toolbox-style checks) run automatically, not left to visual inspection.
4. **Inconsistent scene unit scale (`scale_length`) across assets.** A model can be geometrically perfect and still violate real-world dimension constraints (e.g., railing code minimums) if the unit convention was never pinned to 1 BU = 1 m, because everything still "looks right" relatively.
5. **Boolean solver choice silently changes output correctness.** Fast/Exact/Manifold solvers can each produce a different (sometimes non-manifold, sometimes wrong-normal) result on the *same* inputs; picking one without a manifoldness precondition check produces geometry that fails later in the pipeline, far from the operation that caused it.

## Decisions for the user

- **[Hole-repair escalation policy]** — Which hole-filling method runs by default, and when do we escalate? Options: (1) `bmesh.ops.holes_fill` only (fast, native, fails on non-convex/large holes) — cheap but leaves bad holes unfixed; (2) always escalate to PyMeshLab/MeshFix for anything bmesh can't close — robust but adds an external dependency and runtime cost; (3) Poisson-style reconstruction as a last resort for large/organic holes — best quality, heaviest cost. Recommendation: tiered — bmesh first, MeshFix/PyMeshLab fallback, since MeshFix is purpose-built to "produce a single watertight mesh removing self-intersections/degenerate elements" in one call. Impact: medium. Evidence: **observed** [pymeshfix](https://github.com/pyvista/pymeshfix), [trimesh.repair limitations](https://trimesh.org/trimesh.repair.html).

- **[Boolean solver default]** — Which solver runs for AI-driven cut/union/socket operations? Options: Fast (fastest, breaks on coplanar/non-manifold input); Exact (robust, slower, still has a coplanar-face bug); Manifold-backed (guarantees manifold output on manifold input, but rejects non-manifold input and has its own coplanar bug in the current cycle). Recommendation: gate on a pre-check (is input manifold?) then use Manifold solver when the guarantee applies, Exact otherwise; never default to Fast for anything shipped. Impact: high. Evidence: **observed** [#140590](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/140590), [#82736](https://developer.blender.org/T82736).

- **[Bevel-everywhere policy]** — Given bevel is the highest-cited realism lever, is it auto-applied to every "sharp enough" edge, opt-in per part, or driven by a learned/heuristic classifier of which edges would realistically wear? Options: (1) blanket small bevel on all edges — cheap, but itself reads as artificial per the same sources that flag uniform beveling as a tell; (2) opt-in per asset class; (3) heuristic (convex, exterior-facing, exceeding a dihedral-angle threshold) edges only. Recommendation: (3), since it matches the practitioner guidance that only plausibly-worn exposed edges should bevel. Impact: high (this is the flagged highest-leverage realism fix). Evidence: **observed** [Tripo3D bevel article](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/hd-model-edge-sharpness-and-bevel-realism) (practitioner consensus, not a measured study — see caveat in §b).

- **[Manifoldness export gate]** — Is watertight/manifold validation a hard gate before delivery, or advisory-only? Options: hard gate (blocks export until Open3D/3D-Print-Toolbox checks pass) vs. advisory warning vs. no check. Recommendation: hard gate for any format/use-case implying solidity (3D print, physics, boolean-ready assets); advisory-only acceptable for pure-visual glTF delivery. Impact: high. Evidence: **observed** [3D Print Toolbox](https://projects.blender.org/extensions/print3d_toolbox/wiki/Analyze), [Open3D manifold checks](https://open3d.org/docs/latest/tutorial/Basic/mesh.html).

- **[Scene/asset unit convention]** — Do we lock `scale_length = 1.0` (1 BU = 1 m) globally, or allow per-asset scale with conversion at import? Options: global lock (simplest, breaks on any asset authored differently) vs. per-asset scale + explicit conversion step (more robust, more code). Recommendation: global lock at 1 BU = 1 m with a mandatory conversion/apply-scale step on ingest of any external asset — real-world dimension checks (e.g., railing code numbers) are meaningless otherwise. Impact: medium. Evidence: **observed** [Blender scene units manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/scene_layout/scene/properties.html).

- **[Rotation representation for the agent-facing API]** — What rotation type do AI-agent tool calls set/read? Options: Euler XYZ (human/LLM-readable, gimbal-lock risk on repeated edits), Quaternion (robust, no gimbal lock, opaque to reason about numerically, 180°-interpolation caveat), Axis-Angle (readable for single rotations, awkward to compose). Recommendation: store/compose in quaternion internally, expose Euler only as a display/input convenience layer with explicit order — avoids compounding gimbal-lock errors across many small agent edits. Impact: high (this is a per-tool-call surface, errors compound). Evidence: **observed** [object_rotation_mode_items](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy_types_enum_items/object_rotation_mode_items.html).

- **[Internal up-axis convention]** — Keep Blender's native Z-up internally and convert only at export, or normalize everything to Y-up (glTF-style) internally for multi-target consistency? Options: stay Z-up (native, zero-friction inside Blender, conversion risk pushed to every export) vs. normalize to Y-up internally (extra conversion layer inside Blender itself, but a single tested conversion instead of N ad hoc ones). Recommendation: stay Z-up internally (Blender-native), but centralize axis+handedness conversion in one audited export function per target, given the documented Blender→glTF→Unity handedness chain is a known, repeated bug source. Impact: medium. Evidence: **observed** [UnityGLTF#257](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/issues/257).

- **[Intersection tolerance / intended-vs-unintended clipping policy]** — How do we distinguish a correct socket/embed from an actual clipping bug, given BVH overlap alone can't tell them apart? Options: (1) pure geometric epsilon (flags all overlap beyond a threshold, false-positives on legitimate sockets); (2) semantic tagging (parts declare an expected "embed depth" against specific other parts); (3) class-based heuristic (same-type parts overlapping = bug, different-type = check embed depth). Recommendation: (2), since no library or geometric test can infer design intent — this must be authored. Impact: high (directly determines railing/joinery correctness). Evidence: **inferred** — no source describes an automatic solution; corroborated by FCL's own inability to give reliable non-convex penetration depth (**observed** [FCL#574](https://github.com/flexible-collision-library/fcl/issues/574)).

- **[Railing code numbers: hardcode vs. configurable]** — Do baluster spacing / handrail height defaults use fixed IRC residential numbers, or a configurable per-locale/per-code-body table (IRC vs. IBC vs. non-US codes differ)? Options: hardcode IRC (simple, wrong for commercial/international jobs) vs. configurable table with IRC as default (more setup, correct across contexts). Recommendation: configurable table, IRC residential as the shipped default, since IBC commercial already differs (36in vs 42in guard height) within just the two U.S. codes checked. Impact: medium. Evidence: **observed** [DataDrivenAEC](https://datadrivenaec.com/insights/ibc-guardrail-handrail-requirements).

- **[Delivery vs. archive format]** — What's the canonical working format vs. the delivery format? Options: `.blend` only (full fidelity, not consumable outside Blender) vs. glTF/GLB as delivery + `.blend` as archive vs. USD as the archive/interchange layer for larger multi-asset scenes. Recommendation: `.blend` as the internal working/archive format (preserves modifiers/node graphs for further AI edits), glTF/GLB as the default delivery export (widest real-time/PBR support), with USD considered only if/when multi-asset scene composition becomes a requirement. Impact: medium. Evidence: **observed** [Meshy format comparison](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/meshy-export-formats-glb-fbx-obj-stl-guide).
