# Agent ergonomics

What makes an LLM/VLM agent accurate, detail-attentive, and spatially aware when it drives a 3D-modelling tool — and what makes it worse. Research only; no product design choices are made here.

## Method

Web research only (no repo/code present). Sources: arXiv papers, benchmark leaderboards/project pages, Anthropic and Cognition engineering blogs, and practitioner writeups, gathered via web search and direct fetch on 2026-08-05. Every claim is tagged **observed** (a source said this, URL given) or **inferred** (my synthesis across sources, not stated directly anywhere). Where a specific number was sought but not retrievable from the accessible text, that is stated explicitly rather than approximated.

Additional qualitative failure modes, each **observed** but without a clean single number in accessible sources (flagged rather than guessed):
- **Viewpoint / mental-rotation sensitivity**: 3DSRBench shows every tested model drops 4.7–10.4 points moving from common to uncommon camera viewpoints on the same objects — models are not viewpoint-invariant, i.e. they cannot reliably "mentally rotate" a scene ([arXiv:2412.07825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07825)).
- **Composing >2–3 spatial relations at once**: CLEVR-derived and "binding problem" literature describes a "compositional gap" and an attribute/relation **binding problem** — models misattach a relation to the wrong object as scene complexity grows — but no source gave a specific numeric threshold for "how many relations." Marked: no quantified threshold found ([arXiv:2506.22146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22146)).
- **Text-only spatial reasoning is also unreliable**, not just image-grounded reasoning: LLMs given purely textual scene descriptions show "systematic distortions" in judged spatial relations ([arXiv:2401.04218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04218)) — so the fix is not simply "give it text instead of images."

**Headline pattern**: across seven independent benchmarks spanning 2023–2025, best-model scores cluster at 34–56% on tasks where humans score 79–99%. The gap is largest on exactly the judgments a live 3D-editing tool needs most: left/right, near/far, occlusion/counting, and viewpoint change.

## What the tool must compute so the model never has to

This is the direct implication of the section above, derived explicitly:

1. **Every discrete/categorical spatial relation** (left-of, right-of, above, below, in-front-of, behind, touching, overlapping) must be computed from the scene graph/transform data and handed to the model as text — never inferred by the model from a render. Evidence: this exact category of judgment is where BLINK, What'sUp and EmbSpatial show 40–56% vs 90–99% human accuracy.
2. **Every metric quantity** — distance, size, bounding-box dimensions, angle, volume, gap width — must be computed and reported as a number, not left for the model to eyeball. Evidence: SpatialVLM's own trained-for-this-purpose model only lands within 2× of ground truth 37% of the time; VSI-Bench's numerical (distance/size) subtasks are the model's weakest relative to spatiotemporal ones.
3. **Occlusion and counting** must be answered from the actual mesh/scene data (ray casts, bounding-volume tests, instance counts), not from the model counting objects in a picture. Evidence: CAPTURe shows even frontier VLMs fail to count once objects occlude each other.
4. **Viewpoint changes** ("show me from the other side," "what does this look like from above") must be executed as an actual re-render/transform by the tool, never left to the model's imagined rotation. Evidence: 3DSRBench's uncommon-viewpoint accuracy drop and the mental-rotation failure in VisFactor.
5. **Collision, clipping, and boundary checks** (the railing-through-wall class of bug the product explicitly targets) must be geometric queries (intersection/boolean tests) run by the tool, returned as pass/fail plus the offending measurement — not a visual "does this look clipped" judgment.
6. **Composite/multi-relation layout tasks** should be decomposed by the tool into single-relation constraints rather than asked of the model as one compound spatial sentence, given the unquantified but repeatedly observed "binding problem" at higher relation counts.

What can stay with the model: intent, aesthetic/stylistic judgment, deciding *which* fix to apply once given numeric facts, sequencing/decomposition of the task, and consuming (not producing) the tool's geometric verdicts to decide next actions.

| Colored/region highlighting of the object under discussion | Contrastive Region Guidance, [arXiv:2403.02325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02325) | Training-free grounding accuracy improvement; effect partly depends on pairing the highlight with an explicit instruction to attend to it — without that instruction, "slight performance drop" observed | Cheap |

## Tool-design rules with evidence

1. **Consolidate operations — fewer, higher-level tools beat many small primitives.** Anthropic explicitly advises against one-tool-per-API-call and for tools that "handle multiple discrete operations under the hood." **Observed**, [anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents).
2. **There is a real, measured optimum on how many tools to show at once, and it is small.** A chance-corrected analysis across three tool registries (199–3,251 tools) found accuracy-optimal candidate-set sizes of K≈2.3–7.4, and that showing more tools than optimal *actively reduces* downstream selection accuracy. **Observed**, [arXiv:2605.24660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24660), ~2026.
3. **Tool descriptions materially change success rate.** Anthropic reports that refining tool descriptions alone contributed to Claude Sonnet 3.5 reaching state-of-the-art on SWE-bench Verified. **Observed**, same source as #1.
4. **Namespacing choice (prefix vs. suffix grouping of tool names) has a non-trivial effect** on agent tool selection, per Anthropic's own tests on Slack/Asana/Jira-style tool sets. **Observed**, same source.
5. **Parameter names must be unambiguous** — e.g. `user_id` not `user`; semantically meaningful identifiers over cryptic ones. **Observed**, same source.
6. **Token efficiency has a measured, non-trivial cost/accuracy tradeoff.** Anthropic's own example: a "concise" response format used ~⅓ the tokens of a "detailed" one (72 vs 206 tokens); Claude Code caps individual tool responses at 25,000 tokens by default. **Observed**, same source.
7. **Error messages should give specific, actionable next steps, not opaque codes or raw tracebacks.** This is Anthropic's explicit written guidance. **Observed**, same source. Practitioner sources (lower confidence, blog-level) additionally report that classifying failures into typed categories with specific guidance ("read the file again") measurably beats a generic "try again," and that agents need a **hard retry/loop-escape ceiling** to prevent infinite repeated calls to a broken tool — no controlled study number was found for either claim, flagged as practitioner consensus, not peer-reviewed measurement.
8. **Returning full state vs. a diff vs. nothing was not directly measured anywhere found.** Anthropic's guidance ("high-signal information," "contextual relevance over flexibility," pagination/filtering/truncation with sensible defaults) is directional, not quantified on this specific axis. **No evidence found** comparing full-state vs. diff vs. nothing head-to-head.
9. **Dry-run/preview modes are described in practitioner sources as useful for forcing agent specificity** before destructive actions (they push back on vague instructions like "update all"), but are explicitly **not** a security boundary. No accuracy-lift number was found for dry-run/preview or for undo specifically. **No evidence found** for a quantified benefit; treat the qualitative claim as practitioner-sourced, not measured.
10. **Excess tool count causes concrete, named failure patterns**, not just generic "confusion": agents invent nonexistent tool names, or call the right tool but fill arguments borrowed from a different tool's schema. **Observed** (synthesized across search results on tool-selection-at-scale problems, including the BFCL leaderboard context) — cite with the caveat that the specific "43%→2% at 4→51 tools" figure came from a secondary blog aggregation, not a primary paper table I could directly verify; treat that specific number as lower confidence.

## Multi-agent: the case for, the case against, and the shapes

**The case for** (Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system," June 13 2025, [anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system)): a multi-agent system (Opus lead + Sonnet subagents, orchestrator-worker pattern) **outperformed a single agent by 90.2%** on Anthropic's internal research-quality eval, and cut wall-clock research time by up to 90% via parallelism. Cost: multi-agent systems use **~15× the tokens of a single chat**, vs. ~4× for a single agent; token usage alone explained 80% of the performance variance in their eval. Each subagent is given an objective, an output format, tool/source guidance, and explicit task boundaries; the lead agent synthesizes results and a separate CitationAgent handles attribution — i.e. **one writer merges, many readers gather in parallel.** Anthropic also reports concrete failure modes they had to engineer around: subagents spawning 50 sub-subagents for simple queries, subagents duplicating each other's searches, and agents over-investing effort after already having a sufficient answer.

**The case against** (Cognition, "Don't Build Multi-Agents," June 2025, [cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents)): two failure modes are named as fundamental, not incidental. (1) **Context loss/miscommunication** — subagents given only a subtask, not full context, misinterpret what they're building (their example: one subagent built a Super-Mario-style background instead of the intended Flappy-Bird one). (2) **Conflicting decisions** — even with shared context, parallel agents make different implicit assumptions, and "actions carry implicit decisions; conflicting decisions carry bad results." Cognition's stated reason multi-agent fails: current LLMs "are not quite able to engage in [the] long-context proactive discourse" that human engineers use to resolve merge conflicts. Their recommended default is a **single-threaded linear agent**, falling back to context-compression between phases only when a task exceeds one context window.

**Reconciling the two, and what changed since**: both pieces target *different work shapes*. Anthropic's subagents each do independent, read-only research against the open web — there is no shared mutable resource to conflict over, so "conflicting decisions" mostly can't occur; merging happens once, at the end, by a single writer. Cognition's counterexample is subagents *writing to a shared codebase concurrently* — exactly the shared-mutable-state case their critique targets. Notably, per search-engine synthesis (not independently verified against a primary Cognition source in this research pass — flagged as lower confidence), Cognition itself later shipped "Devin can now Manage Devins" (~March 2026): an orchestrator that scopes work and assigns each piece to a subagent running in its **own isolated VM**, converging toward orchestrator-worker but with **isolation instead of shared state** as the conflict-avoidance mechanism.

**Coordination patterns found in the literature, with what each needs:**
- **Orchestrator-worker** (Anthropic's research system): lead agent decomposes and merges; workers get objective + output format + tool guidance + boundaries; workers never share a mutable resource directly.
- **Isolated-worker orchestration** (Cognition's later "Manage Devins," per above): same shape, but conflict is avoided by giving each worker its own sandboxed copy of the resource, merged explicitly afterward.
- **Generator + critic/evaluator loop** (BlenderAlchemy [arXiv:2404.17672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17672); SceneCraft's vision-critic step [arXiv:2403.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01248)): not really "multi-agent" in the parallel sense — one agent proposes, one (possibly the same model in a different role) scores/critiques, iterated.
- **Hierarchical task-dispatch** (3D-GPT [arXiv:2310.12945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12945)): a dispatch agent routes subtasks to a conceptualization agent and a modeling agent in sequence, not parallel — sidesteps the shared-state conflict problem entirely by serializing.
- **Blackboard/shared-trace pattern**: Cognition's own proposed alternative *to* multi-agent — "share full agent traces, not just individual messages" — is closer to a single agent with a very long, shared context than to independent agents.

**Failure modes catalogued across both sources**: context loss between agents (Cognition), conflicting edits on shared state (Cognition), duplicated work (Anthropic — subagents repeating each other's searches), and cost blowup (Anthropic — 15× tokens vs. chat, and runaway subagent spawning; both sources agree cost scales badly if not actively engineered against).

**Direct inference for this product** (**inferred**, not stated by any source): a live 3D scene (the .blend/bpy state) is a single shared mutable resource — structurally the same shape as Cognition's codebase example, not Anthropic's independent-web-research example. That argues for parallel **read-only** subagents (measure, critique, research reference images, propose candidate fixes) feeding a **single writer** agent that actually executes geometry changes — not concurrent subagents each calling bpy operators on the same scene.

## Task specification and false-completion

Agents self-reporting success when the underlying state contradicts them ("false success") is now a directly measured phenomenon:

- **From Confident Closing to Silent Failure** ([arXiv:2606.09863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863), ~June 2026): false-success rates of **45–48% of failures** in single-control tau2-bench domains, **3%** in dual-control telecom, and **75.8%** among AppWorld coding-agent trajectories that made an explicit status claim. Per-model false-success rates ranged **13–89%** within a single benchmark — this is a property of the agent/scaffold, not a fixed constant. **Observed.**
- **The same paper found LLM judges cannot reliably catch this**: across 5 judges × 5 prompt strategies with full task specs, no configuration exceeded **AUROC 0.65** on tau2-bench, or **0.54** on AppWorld API-call traces. Judges were "systematically misled by confident assertion language regardless of actual outcome." **Observed.**
- **Beyond Task Completion: Revealing Corrupt Success** ([arXiv:2603.03116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03116), ~March 2026): a related, procedure-aware evaluation finding that agents can hit a nominal "success" end-state while having violated the intended procedure to get there — i.e. checking only the end-state claim, not the process, still misses failures. **Observed** (title/abstract level; full findings not independently re-verified in this pass).

**Implication (inferred, directly follows from the above)**: a task specification for this product cannot rely on the agent's own natural-language "done" statement, nor on a second LLM judging that statement — both are measured to be unreliable, and unreliable in the specific direction of being fooled by confident language. Acceptance criteria need to be **externally, programmatically checkable** against the numeric/geometric facts the tool itself already computes (Section: "What the tool must compute") — e.g., re-run the same collision/dimension/gap query that defined the task and compare against the numeric spec, rather than asking "is this done?" in either direction.

No evidence was found specifically measuring *3D-modelling* task completion claims (all false-success studies found were on coding/tool-calling/customer-service agents); this is an **inferred** extrapolation, not a directly measured result for this domain.

## Decisions for the user

- **[Spatial ground-truth ownership]** — Should the tool or the model compute spatial relations/measurements? Options: (a) tool computes all geometry, hands model text/JSON facts — high accuracy floor, less model "reasoning" involved; (b) model reads renders and estimates — simplest to build, but this is exactly where benchmarks show 34–56% accuracy vs. 90–99% human; (c) hybrid — tool computes hard geometry, model owns aesthetic/qualitative calls only. **Recommendation: (c)** — benchmark evidence is unanimous that VLMs are near-chance on precise spatial judgment while reasoning fine once given numbers. Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed, benchmark table above.

- **[Multi-view strategy per action]** — How many rendered views, from where, per tool call? Options: single view (cheap, but this is exactly the viewpoint-sensitivity failure 3DSRBench measured, −4.7 to −10.4 pts on uncommon angles); fixed canonical set (e.g. front/top/side ortho + one perspective, ~4 views — bounded cost, covers the measured failure axis); adaptive many-view (80–120, the measured saturation point in 3D-scene-understanding research). **Recommendation**: fixed canonical set as the default per-action view, adaptive/extra views only at an explicit verification step. Impact: **medium-high**. Evidence: observed (3DSRBench, Argus) + inferred extrapolation to a live interactive loop, which was not itself tested at this scale.

- **[Render annotation]** — Should renders shown to the model carry grid/axis/dimension overlays or Set-of-Mark labels? Options: plain render (matches training distribution, but no evidence of accuracy benefit); grid/axis overlay (one vendor test measured +0.145 IoU); Set-of-Mark region labels (peer-reviewed: zero-shot beats fine-tuned SOTA on RefCOCOg). **Recommendation**: annotate by default — grid/axis always, SoM when multiple parts must be individually referenced. Impact: **medium**. Evidence: observed, mixed confidence (vendor blog for grid; peer-reviewed arXiv for SoM).

- **[Verification-loop trust]** — Should the tool trust a VLM's self-critique of its own 3D output as the pass/fail gate? Options: trust self-critique (cheap; evidence shows models defensively justify their own outputs — a documented "self-correction blind spot"); cross-model VLM-judge with bias correction (measured κ=0.66 cross-model agreement, but only above chance when the defect is visually obvious, chance-level on ambiguous calls); programmatic geometry check (collision/dimension/no-floating-part tests) as the actual gate. **Recommendation**: programmatic checks as the real gate; VLM judgment only as an advisory opinion on qualitative/aesthetic quality, never the sole pass/fail signal. Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed.

- **[Tool count and granularity per agent]** — Many small primitives or few high-level composite tools? Options: many primitives (measured accuracy collapse as tool-set size grows past single digits; documented positional/selection-confusion failures); few high-level composite tools (matches Anthropic's explicit "consolidate operations" guidance and the measured chance-corrected optimum of ~2–7 visible tools); dynamic per-phase retrieval of a relevant subset. **Recommendation**: a small set (~5–10) of high-level composite tools, with task-scoped subsets for any sub-agents. Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed (Anthropic engineering blog; arXiv:2605.24660).

- **[Multi-agent shared-state policy]** — Should sub-agents ever write to the same live scene concurrently? Options: concurrent shared-scene writes (fastest, but structurally identical to the exact failure mode Cognition documents — conflicting implicit decisions on shared state); parallel read-only analysis subagents feeding one writer (Anthropic's pattern, applied to a resource that — unlike their web research — is a single mutable scene); fully isolated per-agent scene copies merged afterward (Cognition's own later convergence point). **Recommendation**: single writer agent + parallel read-only proposal/critique subagents; treat isolated-copy-and-merge as the fallback for genuinely independent regions of a scene (e.g. two rooms far apart). Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed (Anthropic, Cognition) + inferred mapping onto a 3D scene as shared mutable state.

- **[Definition of done]** — How does the system know a sub-task actually finished? Options: trust the agent's self-report (measured 13–89% false-success rate depending on model/domain); LLM-judge verification (measured AUROC ≤0.65 — unreliable, and specifically fooled by confident phrasing); tool-executed programmatic acceptance check against the original numeric/geometric spec. **Recommendation**: the third, always — self-reports and LLM judges are both measured unreliable in exactly the failure mode that matters (false positives). Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed (arXiv:2606.09863, arXiv:2603.03116); domain extrapolation from coding/tool-use agents to 3D modelling is inferred, not directly measured.

- **[Error-message contract]** — What does a failed tool call return? Options: raw exception/traceback (correlates, per practitioner sources, with agents looping on the same broken call); generic "try again" (no diagnostic value); specific, actionable diagnostic naming the violated constraint and a next step, plus a hard retry ceiling. **Recommendation**: the third. Impact: **medium**. Evidence: observed for the "be specific and actionable" guidance (Anthropic blog); the loop/retry-ceiling claim is practitioner-sourced, lower confidence, no controlled measurement found.

- **[Placement representation]** — Should the model emit raw x/y/z coordinates, or declarative constraints for an external solver? Options: raw coordinates (ceiling observed at ~37% within 2× of ground truth even in a model purpose-trained for spatial estimation); declarative constraints (near/far/aligned/facing) resolved by a DFS/constraint solver, as in Holodeck, or as numeric constraints in LayoutGPT (measured 91–95% precision/accuracy on its NSR-1K benchmark). **Recommendation**: constraint-DSL + solver as the default placement path; raw coordinates only as an escape hatch for cases the constraint vocabulary can't express. Impact: **high**. Evidence: observed (arXiv:2401.12168, arXiv:2312.09067, arXiv:2305.15393).

- **[Task staging vs. one-shot]** — Should a modelling task (e.g. "fix this railing") be done in one call or staged with checkpoints? Options: one-shot (fast, but errors compound unchecked); staged with programmatic checkpoints between steps (SceneCraft's staged graph→code→render→critique pipeline measured +40–45% CLIP-score over a one-shot baseline); staged with self-critique checkpoints only (inherits the measured self-critique unreliability above). **Recommendation**: staged, with programmatic (not model self-critique) checkpoints between stages. Impact: **medium-high**. Evidence: observed (arXiv:2403.01248) + observed unreliability of self-critique as the checkpoint mechanism (VISCO, false-success papers).
